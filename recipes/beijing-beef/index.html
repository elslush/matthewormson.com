Beijing beef

Crisp flank steak, bell pepper, and onion in a sweet-tart glaze.

Adapted from Jason Farmer’s recipe

Make ahead

1. Mix the ginger–garlic infusion

Mince the garlic and grate the ginger. Combine with the oil and water; rest for 15 minutes in the refrigerator.

For this step1 tbsp garlic; 2 tsp fresh ginger; 1 tsp vegetable oil; 1 tsp water.

Hot oil · 275°F / 135°C

2. Make the chili infusion

Moisten the flakes with the water in a large heatproof bowl. Heat the oil to 275°F / 135°C. Pour it in slowly; the mixture foams. Refrigerate both infusions and use within 4 days, or freeze.

For this step¼ cup crushed red pepper flakes; 2 tbsp water; 2 tbsp vegetable oil.

Refrigerate at least 45 minutes

3. Marinate the beef

Slice the flank steak ¼ inch thick across the grain. Massage baking soda into the strips. Mix in the soy sauce, water, and beef base; cover and refrigerate.

For this step1 lb flank steak; 2 tsp Chinese light soy sauce; ½ tsp baking soda; 2 tsp water; 1 tsp roasted beef base.

Timers are reminders; check the food before continuing.
For today’s half-batch

4. Mix the sauce

Warm everything except the cornstarch and xanthan until smooth, without boiling. Cool, then whisk in the starch and gum.

For this step2 ½ tbsp granulated sugar; 2 ½ tbsp distilled white vinegar; 2 ½ tbsp water; 1 ½ tbsp Chinese light soy sauce; 1 ½ tbsp tomato paste; 2 tsp dark corn syrup; ¼ tsp MSG; ⅛ tsp citric acid; ¼ tsp Morton table salt; 1 tsp cornstarch; 1/16 tsp xanthan gum.

Press the coating firmly

5. Coat the beef

Combine the dry ingredients. Separately whisk the egg whites until frothy, then toss with the beef. Press each strip into the dry mixture.

For this step7 tbsp cornstarch; 3 ½ tbsp fine white rice flour; 3 ½ tbsp all-purpose flour; 1 tbsp nonfat dry milk powder; ¼ tsp white pepper; 2 egg whites.

First fry · 325°F / 165°C

6. Cook the beef through

Heat frying oil with the tallow to 325°F / 165°C. Fry in small batches until the meat reaches 145°F / 63°C; rest 3 minutes. This cook-through step replaces the source’s partial first fry.

For this stepAs needed vegetable oil; 1 ½ tbsp beef tallow.

At least 6 hours in the freezer

7. Chill, freeze, and divide

Drain, then refrigerate promptly in a shallow single layer; do not wait for room temperature. Get it into the refrigerator or freezer within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F / 32°C). Freeze in a single layer for at least 6 hours. Bag half for another meal.

Second fry · 375°F / 190°C

8. Crisp half of the beef

Keep half frozen for another meal. Fry the other half straight from frozen, in small batches, at 375°F / 190°C until crisp and 165°F / 74°C inside. The source suggests 4–5 minutes; check temperature.

Keep the vegetables crisp

9. Stir-fry the aromatics and vegetables

Heat the wok oil; stir-fry the infusions for 10 seconds. Add the pepper and onion; stir-fry for 30–45 seconds.

For this step1 tbsp vegetable oil; 2 tsp prepared ginger–garlic infusion; 2 tsp prepared chili infusion; 2 oz red bell pepper; 2 oz yellow onion.

Finish in 15–20 seconds

10. Glaze and serve

Stir the sauce, add it all to the wok, and boil until thickened. Toss in the crisp beef just until coated; serve with rice.

Source & notes

Ingredients and cooking technique adapted from Jason Farmer’s Beijing beef recipe. Visit his page for the original recipe, photographs, and video. This adaptation has not been tested in Matthew’s kitchen.

The method here cooks the meat through before freezing and reheats it to 165°F / 74°C. USDA guidance advises cooking meat and poultry completely in one session. See also USDA cooking temperatures and prompt chilling guidance.

For the reserved half of the beef, make another batch of sauce, vegetables, and wok ingredients when ready to finish it. Do not double the sauce and use it all on today’s half.
