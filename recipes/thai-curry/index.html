01Prep

Get everything ready

Slice the chicken, eggplant, bell pepper, and Thai chilies. Drain the bamboo shoots, grate the palm sugar, and set out the basil and lime leaves. Start your rice according to its package or rice-cooker directions.

↗

Look for thisThe cream goes from a loose puddle to a thicker, bubbling base.

02Medium heat

Start with the coconut cream

Scoop the thick cream from the tops of the coconut-milk cans into a large, deep pan. Add the torn makrut lime leaves. Heat gently, stirring, until the cream bubbles and thickens. Keep the rest of the coconut milk for later.

↗

Look for thisA spatula drawn through the paste leaves a visible trail.

2 more photos from this step
03Stir frequently

Cook the curry paste

Add all the red curry paste to the reduced coconut cream. Stir it through and cook until fragrant, well combined, and noticeably thicker. Keep it moving so the paste does not catch on the pan.

↗

Look for thisThe thick curry base loosens into a smooth orange sauce.

2 more photos from this step
04Build the sauce

Add chilies, eggplant, and coconut milk

Stir the sliced Thai chilies into the paste. Add the eggplant rounds and all the remaining coconut milk. Stir until the paste is evenly dispersed and the eggplant is coated in sauce.

↗

Look for thisThe sauce looks slightly thicker and the eggplant begins to soften.

05Gentle bubbling

Let the sauce reduce

Bring the sauce to a steady bubble, then adjust the heat so it cooks without scorching. Let it reduce slightly and give the eggplant time to soften, stirring occasionally. Allow roughly 10–20 minutes and use the texture as your guide.

06Gentle simmer

Add chicken and the final ingredients

Lower the heat to a simmer. Add the chicken, bamboo shoots, bell pepper, Thai basil, grated palm sugar, fish sauce, and tamarind concentrate. Stir everything into the sauce so the chicken cooks evenly.

↗

Look for thisMeasure the chicken itself; the sauce temperature does not confirm that the meat is done.

1 more photo from this step
07Check doneness

Simmer until the chicken is cooked

Cover and simmer gently, stirring occasionally, until the chicken is cooked through and the eggplant is tender. Start checking after about 10–15 minutes; larger pieces may need longer. Check the thickest chicken pieces with a food thermometer: they must reach 165°F / 74°C. Taste the finished sauce and adjust to your preference.

12:00Check the food; time is only a guide.
↗

Look for thisThe finished bowl: creamy red curry, tender vegetables, and rice underneath.

08Ready to eat

Spoon over rice

Remove the whole lime leaves and any tough basil stems. Spoon the curry over hot cooked rice, with plenty of sauce, chicken, and vegetables in each bowl.

Chicken should reach 165°F / 74°C in its thickest part. USDA temperature guidance.
